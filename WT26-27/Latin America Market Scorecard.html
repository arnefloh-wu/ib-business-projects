Case study
Market screening · composite indicator

Which Latin American market should a European premium coffee brand enter first?

We ranked six markets on 45 public indicators across the six PESTEL dimensions. Change the weights or the normalisation below and the ranking is recalculated from the raw data.

One weighted score, built in five steps

Step 1

Collect

45 indicators per country: 5 political, 10 economic, 10 social, 10 technological, 5 environmental, 5 legal. Latest year, one public source each.

Step 2

Normalise

Min-max: 100·(x−min)/(max−min), reversed where lower is better. Checked against T-scores: 50+10·z.

Step 3

Weight

One weight per dimension, fixed before the data were collected and split equally among its indicators.

Step 4

Aggregate

Score = Σ weight × dimension mean. A weighted sum, so a strength can offset a weakness.

Step 5

Stress-test

Re-rank under four alternative weight sets and with T-scores. A rank that holds is a result you can decide on.

What the method can and cannot carry

The model works for any screening that compares a fixed set of markets on many indicators. Swap the indicators, set the weights before you look at the data, and report the rank range rather than a single rank.

Limits. With six countries, min-max stretches small gaps, and one outlier (Mexico’s tourist arrivals, Brazil’s coffee market) can push the others towards zero. A weighted sum lets a large market offset weak institutions, so knock-out conditions such as a prohibitive tariff need a separate filter. Some indicators are proxies, and data years range from 2015 to 2026.

Data: public sources (World Bank WDI, WGI and LPI, UN agencies, OECD, Transparency International, WIPO, Yale EPI, ND-GAIN, EU Access2Markets, Euromonitor and Statista via a university library, among others), collected by student groups in the course International Business Projects, WU Vienna, winter term 2026/27.
