Composite indicator · market screening

Which Latin American market should a Viennese coffee roaster enter first?

Six markets, 45 public indicators across six PESTEL dimensions, one weighted score. Change the weights or the normalisation and the ranking recalculates from the raw data.

How the score is built

Step 1

Collect

45 indicators per country (5 political, 10 economic, 10 social, 10 technological, 5 environmental, 5 legal), latest year, one public source each.

Step 2

Normalise

Min-max: 100·(x−min)/(max−min), reversed where lower is better. Check with T-scores: 50+10·z.

Step 3

Weight

Weights per dimension, fixed before data collection and split equally among its indicators.

Step 4

Aggregate

Score = Σ weight × dimension mean. A weighted sum, so strengths offset weaknesses.

Step 5

Stress-test

Re-rank under four alternative weight sets and with T-scores. A stable rank is a robust result.

Using the method elsewhere

The model is generic: any market screening that compares a fixed set of countries on many indicators can reuse it. Swap the indicators, set the weights before looking at the data, and report the rank range rather than a single rank.

Known limits. With six countries, min-max stretches small gaps and one outlier (Mexico's tourist arrivals, Brazil's coffee market) can squeeze the others towards zero. A weighted sum lets a large market offset weak institutions; knock-out conditions such as a prohibitive tariff need a separate filter. Some indicators are proxies, and data years range from 2015 to 2026.

Data: values collected from public sources (World Bank WDI, WGI and LPI, UN agencies, OECD, Transparency International, WIPO, Yale EPI, ND-GAIN, EU Access2Markets, Euromonitor and Statista via a university library, among others) by student groups in the course International Business Projects, WU Vienna, winter term 2026/27, status November 2026. Weights and alternative weight sets as fixed by the course instructor.
